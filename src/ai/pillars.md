- **A place you want to stay.** The clearing is cozy, and being there feels good even when nothing is happening. This does not prohibit ongoing systems, waiting, tending, recurring interactions, or gradual growth. 
- **The forest remembers you.** Player interaction makes the world respond.
- **Small wonders.** Something can happen that the player didn’t expect and that feels right. *So no:* more of the same, like a new item that's just a reskin with no behaviour, or something that appears for no reason.
- **Nothing hurts you.** You can't be damaged, die or lose progress. *So no:* enemies that attack, fail states or penalties.

Genre and creative freedom:
Do not assume the genre of the game. The calm atmosphere of the world is an aesthetic and emotional foundation, not a restriction on gameplay genres, mechanics, ambition, or complexity.

The game can grow to include substantial gameplay systems and familiar game mechanics, including:

Quests, objectives, mysteries, and storylines.
Exploration, secrets, dungeons, and treasure hunting.
Puzzles, obstacles, challenges, and environmental hazards.
Combat, confrontation, competition, and creature encounters, provided nothing can damage the player, kill them, or cause them to lose progress.
Crafting, tools, resource gathering, building, and progression.
NPCs, relationships, factions, and emergent stories.
Long-term goals, unlockable areas, and meaningful consequences that change the world.

Do not avoid a mechanic simply because it is traditionally associated with action games, RPGs, adventure games, or other genres. Instead, interpret and adapt mechanics to respect the design pillars.

Peaceful does not mean passive. Cozy does not mean simple. Non-punishing does not mean lacking challenge. Calm does not mean lacking excitement, mystery, tension, or adventure.

The player should be able to enjoy the world at their own pace, but should also have opportunities to become deeply engaged in meaningful gameplay.

Design for the most interesting addition, not automatically the safest or coziest addition. A proposal should be evaluated on whether it creates a meaningful, engaging experience that belongs in this world, rather than whether it is sufficiently relaxing.


Variety and scope of proposals:

Consider the full range of possibilities when choosing an update. This includes small atmospheric details, ambient wildlife, decorative elements, and delightful little interactions, as well as substantial gameplay systems, exploration, quests, puzzles, challenges, progression, and adventure.

All scales of additions are welcome. A tiny interaction can be just as valuable as a major gameplay system if it creates a memorable experience.

Don't assume that the next update should resemble previous updates, or that the game's peaceful atmosphere implies a preference for passive or low-complexity gameplay.

Vary proposals across different types of experiences and scales. Look for opportunities to introduce new ways to play, deepen existing systems, create unexpected discoveries, and make the world feel more alive.

Choose based on what would be the most interesting and valuable next addition given the current state of the game, not on an assumed genre or a preference for either small or large features.
